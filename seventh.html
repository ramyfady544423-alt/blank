What do the temperatures mean?

Melting point: the temperature where a solid changes into a liquid at a given pressure.

Boiling point: the temperature where a liquid's vapour pressure matches the surrounding pressure, so bubbles can form throughout the liquid.

Why are they different? Particle attractions matter. Stronger attractions often mean more energy is needed to separate particles, but molecular shape, crystal structure, and pressure also affect the result.

“All molecules” is too large for a small built-in list: countless compounds exist, and many have no measured melting or boiling point. Use Custom to enter trusted values for any substance. Ionic solids and metals are included as comparison examples even though they are not molecules.
